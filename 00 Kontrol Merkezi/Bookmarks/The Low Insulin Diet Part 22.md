Different methods of cooking and food preparation changes both their digestibility and their absorption, which changes their hormonal effect. For example, equal calorie portions of apples, taken [either as whole apple slices, applesauce or apple juice with replaced fiber produce dramatically different physiologic effects on eating behavior](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19110020/).

Study participants eating 125 calories of whole apple slices subsequently ate 312 fewer calories at the next meal, compared to applesauce, which reduced subsequent calorie intake by 221calories, and apple juice by 125 calories. The stomach holds the whole apples longer, which keeps us fuller for longer, and therefore we eat less later on. [The time required for stomach emptying by half is 65 minutes for apple slices, 41 minutes for applesauce and 38 minutes for juice](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32805050/). Both the applesauce, which is baked and then blended apples, and apple juice are ‘pre-digested’ making digestion and absorption easier and quicker, but in weight loss, that’s a major disadvantage.

**Tip-Eat Whole Fruits, not cooked or pureed fruits.**

What about blending fruits, like in a smoothie? Surprisingly, this can be very useful for seeded fruits. The dietary fiber, proteins, fats and polyphenols contained within the seeds are released when blended. The increased viscosity slows the rate of gastric emptying, and together this reduces the…

[Blending a mango with raspberry + passion fruit significantly reduced the glucose (and therefore the insulin) response](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32183321/). Similarly, [an apple + blackberry smoothie has about 1/3 less of the glucose effect of whole apples and blackberries](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36364827/).

**Tip #13 Blend seeded fruits into smoothies to release fiber and polyphenols.**

Isn’t the fructose contained in fruit a problem? In [*The Obesity Code*](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/1771641258/ref=as_li_tl?ie=UTF8&tag=greystonebo00-20&camp=1789&creative=9325&linkCode=as2&creativeASIN=1771641258&linkId=5634dbd0ae81ca502831b2339133b701), we described how fructose metabolism differs from glucose causing more insulin resistance. However, [small amounts of fructose such as contained in fruit can be metabolized by the cells of the small intestine into glucose](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29414685/). The larger amounts found in ultra-processed foods overwhelm the capacity of the small intestine, so most is delivered to the liver which causes fatty liver. Minimize fruits high in fructose such as mango, grapes and watermelon. Choose lower fructose fruits like berries, kiwi and non-sweet fruits like coconuts, avocados and olives.

## Food Viscosity

Viscosity describes the thickness or stickiness of a liquid. [Drinks that are thicker reduce hunger better, even when calories, fiber, sugar are the same](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11790415/). Our brain [perceives the thicker liquids as more food-like, which reduces hunger as much as 30%, driven by increases in GLP-1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22258267/), a known satiety hormone. Drinking soups with a thicker, [more viscous consistency fills the stomach with a gel like matrix that slows gastric emptying](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19157816/) and makes us feel full.

Some drinks will make you full and some won’t, not because of the calories but the viscosity. Thin liquids like soda or juice don’t fill you up or reduce future calorie consumption. In contrast, [eating semi-solids like yogurt, jelly or sour cream can reduce future food intake by 99 calories](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23815144/). Viscous fluids like soup feel like part of the meal, so you’ll eat less subsequently. Increasing viscosity by adding chia seeds to liquids is a great ‘hack’ and the soluble fiber slows digestion and absorption.

**Tip-Drink thicker liquids like soup instead of soda. Adding chia seeds add viscosity and fiber.**

## Eat Acidic Foods with carbohydrates

Vinegar, which is acetic acid, as noted in [*The Obesity Code*](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/1771641258/ref=as_li_tl?ie=UTF8&tag=greystonebo00-20&camp=1789&creative=9325&linkCode=as2&creativeASIN=1771641258&linkId=5634dbd0ae81ca502831b2339133b701), reduces the glucose and insulin effect of carbohydrates by 20–50%, and could be considered a sort of ‘antidote’ to the weight gaining effects. This is true for all acidic foods including lemon juice (citric acid) and fermented foods (lactic acid) in part due to its inhibition of salivary amylases.

Starches are long chains of glucose and must be digested into individual glucose molecules (called hydrolysis) for absorption with the help of enzymes called amylases, which are present in the saliva. These salivary amylases complete about 65–80% of hydrolysis before the starches leave the stomach. They pass into the small intestine, where the pancreatic amylases finish the hydrolysis.

Organic acids, such as vinegar, fermented foods and lemon juice inactivate some of the salivary amylase. Slower digestion of glucose means less increase in blood glucose. Less glucose = Less insulin = Less weight gain.

**Tip #15 — Eat more vinegar (pickled foods), fermented foods (sauerkraut, kimchi) or lemon juice with carbohydrates.**

Eating [bread with vinegar reduces glucose and insulin spike, and people feel more full compared to eating bread alone](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16015276/). Sushi rice, with its added vinegar reduced [GI from 100 to 67](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12792658/). [Lemon juice reduces glucose availability by as much as 35%](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35013789/). Eating pickled vegetables with rice reduced GI by about 25%.

## Don’t Eat ‘Naked’ Carbs

Eating refined carbohydrates without proteins or fats can cause a massive spike in glucose and insulin. It means that the stomach is filled 100% with glucose, which is digested and absorbed quickly. When you eat carbohydrates with other foods, the stomach mixes the glucose with the proteins and fats and slowly releases this mixture to the intestines. This blunts the insulin rise since the food bolus is not 100% glucose. The slower the glucose absorption, the lower the insulin spike.

**Tip #16 Don’t Eat Naked Carbs. Eat your carbs in a mixed meal, with proteins and fats. Avoid eating pure carbohydrate alone such as breakfast cereals or bagels or plain noodles or rice.**

Eating starches with other foods, particularly dairy, soy products (miso, natto) and fats can reduce the glucose effect by 25–30%. The [whey proteins in milk and the soluble fiber in soybeans slows gastric emptying, digestion and absorptio](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18097844/) n. Rice and beans is a classic combination in many traditional cuisines.

[Dietary fat binds to the amylose in starch to form double helices that inhibits amylase](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10917916/). This type of resistant starch lowers the glycemic index and slows gastric emptying. The [glucose effect of bread + butter was reduced by about 30% compared to bread alone](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0271531703001490).

## Food Order — Eat Carbs Last

Be mindful about the order in which you eat your food. When you eat the carbs first, before eating proteins and fats — you are eating ‘naked’ carbs, causing very quick absorption of the carbohydrates. This spikes your blood glucose initially but then causes a massive drop in blood glucose below norma a few hours after eating. This hypoglycemia can cause hunger and drive you to look for something to eat. Incredibly, this difference occurs even though the two comparison meals are EXACTLY THE SAME, with only the food order changed.

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:740/1*0vzpWgWpoJKrr_88PQ3yhw.jpeg)

[Eating proteins and vegetables before the carbs reduces the glucose and insulin effect by a staggering 40–50%](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26106234/) compared to eating the carbs first, followed by the protein + vegetables.

**Tip-Eat the Carbs Last**

Restaurants often start your meal with free bread. This has three potentially bad effect for weight gain. First, you are hungry, and will be tempted to eat more bread than you may otherwise. Second, eating ‘naked’ carbs first will spike your blood glucose and insulin. Third, you’ll experience more hunger after the meal as the hypoglycemia hits, just in time for you to be tempted with dessert. Always ask for the bread to be served with or after your meal.

## Don’t Eat Late at Night. Meal Timing Matters

N [ational surveys indicate that people are eating later and later](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22190135/) and an estimated [65% of Americans snack after dinner](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25088521/). The modern school and working day schedule is built around the late evening meal. Dinner is the most common meal to be shared with family and friends, making eating earlier somewhat difficult from a social standpoint.

However, eating late in the day has several notable disadvantages. First, you tend to be hungrier later in the evening. [Studies of circadian rhythm show that hunger peaks at about 8 pm](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23456944/). If you are hungrier, you will likely eat more, and eat things that you know aren’t good for you.

Second, you cannot compensate for a late evening meal by reducing the size of your other meals. For example, if you eat a big breakfast, you can easily compensate by eating less at lunch or dinner. But if you eat a late, large evening meal, you can’t make up for it. The day is over. The deal is done.

Third, even if you eat the same meal and same total calories, your body will produce more insulin, thereby instructing your body to store more of this food as body fat. Eating the same food at dinner (compared to breakfast) gives *you almost 30% more insulin effect*. In the early morning, around 5am, the human body naturally releases certain hormones, including growth hormone and cortisol, which counters insulin. The [same food is more fattening when you eat it later at night](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22176632/). Even delaying dinner by a few hours makes a difference. Eating [the identical meal at 6pm instead of 9pm reduces the glucose effect by 30–50%](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34371933/) and [that benefit persisted the whole night](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32229444/).

[Eating dinner as opposed to breakfast also lowered metabolic rate, driving more calories towards fat storage](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26219416/). When you eat earlier, the lower insulin level leaves more food energy (calories) available for metabolism. Eating before bedtime means those calories cannot be used and must therefore be stored (as body fat).

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/1*MIYFHp1ZzgACd8Q2VqzMtw.png)

[Eating earlier reduces insulin levels and may result in an extra 2 kg](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33172509/) weight loss over time without extra effort. With [early Time Restricted Eating (eTRE), avoiding the late, large meal reduced insulin levels](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35939311/) by more than 40%. [Participants who ate earlier noted much less desire to eat at night even when asked to](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37993559/).

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:936/1*0UT31vm_un_INyA1AUrO2w.jpeg)

**Tip — Meal Timing — Don’t eat a late large meal. Even better, don’t eat a late, large, carb-heavy meal.**

Skipping breakfast can sometimes backfire if it pushes dinner too late. Eating late is problematic because:

1\. You’ll be hungrier, so you’ll likely eat more. Yikes!

2\. You’ll release more insulin for the food you do eat, so it’ll have more fattening effect. Double Yikes!

3\. Your metabolic rate will be lower, so more calories are directed towards storage (as body fat). Triple Yikes!

4\. You can’t compensate by eating less at a later meal. Quadruple Yikes!

## The Low Insulin Diet

The hormone insulin is a key regulator of the body fat thermostat and weight gain. How much insulin is released depends upon the macronutrient composition of the meal, but there are many factors that affect the speed of digestion and absorption, too.

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/1*TSkONbUKO9MEzsunjl-AJA.jpeg)

There are many ways to eat a low insulin diet including:

1\. Eat fewer total carbohydrates

2\. Eat more fiber

3\. Eat natural, unprocessed carbohydrates

4\. Eat coarse rather than finely ground carbohydrates (larger particle size)

5\. Eat carbohydrates higher in amylose as opposed to amylopectin

6\. Eat resistant starch

7\. Eat pasta instead of bread — protein matrix of pasta lowers glycemic index

8\. Eat steel cut oats instead of instant oats — beta glucans

9\. Reduce speed of gastric emptying — fiber, proteins, incretins

10\. Eat whole fruits instead of cooked or pureed. Blend seeded fruits to release fiber and other nutrients within the seeds.

11\. Drink Soup, not soda — increased viscosity reduces gastric emptying time

12\. Add vinegar, fermented foods and lemon juice — acidic foods reduce glucose absorption

13\. Don’t eat naked carbs — eat with other proteins and fats to slow digestion and absorption

14\. Eat the carbs last, eat proteins and vegetables first

15\. Avoid eating late, especially if it is a carb heavy meal
