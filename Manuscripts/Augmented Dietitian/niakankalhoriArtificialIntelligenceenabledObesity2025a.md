Methods: "A thorough review of 6,351 articles, focusing on AI predictions for obesity in cohort studies, was conducted up to March 2024 across databases including PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Scienc"
Findings: "Machine learning was predominantly used (95 % of studies), mostly employing supervised learning techniques. Algorithms like random forest (RF) (18 %), linear regression (18 %), and stochastic gradient boosting (GBM) (14 %) were common. Top-performing models were noted for k-means (accuracy of 0.977), artificial neural networks (AUC of 0.99), GBM (specificity of 0.95 and sensitivity of 0.65), RF (RMSE of 0.146), and least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (r-squared of 0.684).Findings indicate that AI algorithms can predict obesity; however, further research is needed to assess their effectiveness in analyzing obesity-related data and examine most advanced AI methods"
Conclusions: "The findings from this review highlight important implications for clinical practice and public health policy. AI models can facilitate early detection of obesity risk, enabling timely interventions that may prevent conditions like type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular diseases [28,56]. By using predictive tools, healthcare providers can implement personalized prevention strategies, improving long-term health outcomes and reducing healthcare system burdens. Additionally, dietitians and nutritionists can leverage AI to identify high-risk individuals, allowing for tailored dietary and lifestyle interventions that address risks proactively.  For policymakers, AI-driven models can assist in designing effective obesity prevention programs by identifying high-risk populations and optimizing resource allocation. This approach enables better monitoring of public health initiatives, facilitating data-driven adjustments to strategies aimed at reducing obesity prevalence.To enhance obesity prediction using AI, research should integrate multi-omics data—genomics, proteomics, metabolomics, and microbiomics—to uncover the biological mechanisms of obesity. This integration will support personalized intervention strategies with tailored lifestyle and dietary recommendations, improving health outcomes. Collaboration among researchers, clinicians, dietitians, and policymakers through data-sharing platforms is crucial for developing robust AI models"
