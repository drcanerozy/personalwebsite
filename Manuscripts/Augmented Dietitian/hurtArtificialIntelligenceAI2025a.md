Methods: "This retrospective study evaluated four LLM‐based AIAsChatGPT (OpenAI), OpenEvidence (OpenEvidence Inc), Gemini (Google, Google DeepMind), and Copilot (Microsoft Corporation)—using five complex CN cases from our nutrition support service. Each AIA was queried with patient‐specific CN questions. Findings: "All AIAs produced clinically appropriate responses, with Gemini scoring highest in relevance (4.04) and clarity (4.16). Overall satisfaction scores ranged from 3.08 (Copilot) to 3.84 (Gemini). Citation quality and originality of insights varied and were generally limited, and no consistent differences in performance were observed across the five cases among the four AIAsThe primary finding of this study is that a wide range of LLM‐based AIAs performed well when presented with full, complex CN clinical vignettes from a high‐acuiNST at a tertiary academic medical center. Despite the complexity of the CN cases, all AIAs generated responses that physician nutrition specialists deemed clinically reasonable and reflective of care plans they have implemented in real‐world settings. Conclusions: "LLM‐based AIAs can reliably replicate expert reasoning in CN. Although not yet a source of novel clinical insights, the true potential of this approach may lie in its application among physicians without specialized expertise in CN, helping to bridge existing knowledge gaps in nutrition care. Presenting full clinical cases, as shown in this study, could support AIA‐enabled e‐consultation in the future, thereby addressing gaps in CN education.Ensuring the accuracy of model citations and continued evaluation across diverse care settings will be essential steps in realizing the full potential of generative AIAs in CN."
Limitations: "However, all clinical data were concurrently gathered and synthesized into a clinical vignette with the generation of an appropriate clinical dilemma by expert nutrition support physicians. This makes it difficult to assess whether these LLM AIAs would be able to gather this information in a live interaction with patients or through review of an electronic medical record. This is a significant limitation because a physician without significant CN training could easily miss the impact of optimal nutrition support for the patient they are managing and never ask the LLM for appropriate guidance in that area.  Future studies need to explore the role of LLM AIAs as CDM aids for generalist physicians who lack CN training or trainees, particularly in low‐resource settings. Future studies will also explore live clinical data integration to test performance and usability of these AIAs within real‐world clinical workflows."
