there was this girl i knew.

not romantically. just someone in my orbit for a while. smart. funny. seemed like she had it together.

and the more time i spent around her the more i stopped being able to just be around her.

because i couldn’t stop seeing it.

the way she’d fish for reassurance and then immediately deflect when it came. wanting the validation but unable to sit with it because somewhere underneath she didn’t believe she’d earned it.

the way she’d bring up her ex three times in a single conversation, completely unprompted. still circling that wound.

the way her energy would shift to match whoever she was talking to. chameleon-like. no stable centre. just adapting to the room to maximize approval.

and i want to be clear. i wasn’t judging her for it.

everyone is doing some version of this. i’m doing some version of this. we’re all running patterns we didn’t consciously write.

i do it. you do it.

but the problem is i couldn’t not see it.

i couldn’t be present with her because part of me was always above the conversation, taking notes.

the awareness started to ruin it.

analyzing instead of experiencing. understanding instead of feeling.

that’s the specific kind of loneliness nobody warns you about.

you gain the ability to see clearly.

and you lose the ability to just be there.

6:23am as i write these words.

herbal tea instead of coffee. tastes like grass clippings but the caffeine would guarantee i’m wired.

frank sinatra playing in the background.

been wrestling with something for months now. how awareness, the thing everyone chases, the whole “wake up, see clearly, understand yourself and others” pipeline, has made certain things harder rather than easier.

nobody talks about this part.

let’s talk about it.

## THE PARADOX OF SELF AWARENESS

*"There is no coming to consciousness without pain."* — Carl Jung

when you start observing yourself, you split into two.

there’s the you that’s living. and there’s the you that’s watching you live.

and here’s where the problem lies.

these two people cannot fully occupy the same moment at the same time.

think about the last conversation you had where you were monitoring how you were coming across. were you actually in it? fully present? or were you floating slightly above it, narrating?

“i’m being too quiet right now.”

“that pause was awkward.”

“do they actually like me or are they just being polite?”

“maybe i should have said something different”

that’s the split.

you left the moment to observe the moment. and by the time you tried to come back, the moment had already moved on without you.

do this long enough and you stop being able to tell which version is actually you.

the one living or the one watching.

the feeling or the analysis of the feeling.

the person or the personality type.

watch yourself closely enough for long enough and you almost become a performance for yourself.

the self you’re performing for is also you, which means you’re never fully off stage.

the performance becomes the person.

and that’s before you start applying this to other people.

## THE FEEDBACK LOOP

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Evzu!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F4fbd0820-b78e-4e6e-b568-b52151220879_1672x557.png)

here’s what’s actually happening neurologically.

normally you experience things directly.

there is minimal gap between the event and your response. you’re just living. present tense, no commentary.

but awareness inserts an observer layer.

now you’re not just experiencing. you’re watching yourself experience. analyzing the experience while it’s happening.

and here’s where it gets strange. the observation changes the experience itself.'

physicists ran into this with quantum particles. the double slit experiment.

the act of observing a particle’s position collapses its wave function. changes what it actually does.

the observer and the observed are no longer separate.

your consciousness does the same thing when you observe it.

say you’re at a party. normally you’d just be there. interacting. feeling things. moving through the evening.

with awareness? you’re simultaneously at the party and above the party, watching yourself be at the party.

meta-cognition in real time. some spooky shit.

the observation creates distance so now you’re no longer fully inside the experience because part of you is standing back, running analysis.

this is called cognitive distancing in psychology.

therapists teach it as a skill. step back from your thoughts. examine them. don’t be swept away.

and it works. distance from your thoughts reduces their grip on you. genuinely useful for anxiety, emotional regulation, for not being completely controlled by every feeling that passes through.

but here’s what they don’t tell you.

you can’t turn it off once it develops.

you meditate and practice watching your thoughts. you do therapy and track your patterns. you study psychology and learn to recognize the architecture underneath behaviour.

and your brain starts doing it automatically. all the time. without your permission.

you’ve trained a part of your consciousness to watch the rest of your consciousness.

and that watching never fully stops.

not at the party or in conversation. not even when you’re alone.

you’ve created a permanent observer inside your own head.

and observers, by definition, cannot fully participate in what they’re observing.

that’s the specific curse.

that you can’t stop seeing them.

## THE MOVIE AND THE PARTY

the movie problem is real and once it hits you it hits you forever.

try watching a film now the way you used to. before you understood narrative structure and ideological framing and whose interests get centred in stories.

the character arc crafted to make you sympathize with a specific worldview. the romance subplot selling an impossible standard. the hero’s journey repackaged to make a particular kind of power seem natural.

you see the construction and the invisible hand guiding each choice.

i used to genuinely disappear into films. hours gone. fully inside the story. feeling what the characters felt.

now there’s always a part of me standing behind the screen, pointing.

i can still enjoy things don’t get me wrong. it’s not like i’m a stick in the mud pointing out everything and acting like it’s some massive conspiracy and i’m above it all.

but the total surrender isn’t available anymore. being fully influenced requires not seeing the mechanism doing the influencing. and once you see it, that’s seen.

parties are the same.

i walk into a social gathering now and everyone becomes transparent in a way that used to take months to notice.

the guy dominating every conversation in the corner. performing confidence to compensate for something he’s been carrying since he was too small to fight back.

the person documenting everything. can’t fully experience the night without external validation that it’s worth experiencing.

the friend group nodding along with everything because disagreement might fracture the only social structure currently holding their loneliness together.

everyone performing.

and you’re supposed to participate. add your own performance to the collection. pretend it’s connection.

but you can’t.

because authentic connection requires some degree of illusion.

requires not seeing all the wounds and performances and patterns running underneath.

requires just being with people without your brain running diagnostics the entire time.

and it very well could be the case that people look at me the same way as the guy who overthinks everything and is an over analyzer.

a performance to some degree too.

that’s where the self awareness aspect comes in because you realize the same way you look at other people could be the exact same way they look at you.

but once you’ve trained your brain to analyze, it won’t stop when you want it to.

## THE SELF AWARENESS SPIRAL

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!HmuQ!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fc984faf2-84cf-441e-8397-21706e1da8d6_735x261.jpeg)

self awareness gets worse when you turn it inward.

because you don’t just see the patterns in other people.

you start seeing them in yourself. in real time.

can’t just feel something. have to understand why you’re feeling it. what triggered it. what pattern it belongs to. what wound it’s protecting.

you become hyperaware of your own performances while you’re still performing them.

performing confidence while knowing you’re performing it.

seeking validation while being fully aware you’re seeking it.

running a defence mechanism while watching yourself run it.

it doesn’t make any of it stop. it just makes you conscious of it while it’s happening.

you can’t even hide from yourself anymore.

stuck in a recursive loop of awareness aware of itself.

and it is genuinely exhausting.

## THE NOSTALGIA FOR IGNORANCE

*"We see the world not as it is, but as we are."* — Anais Nin

i miss not knowing this stuff.

miss being able to watch something and just feel it. go somewhere and just be there. be around someone and just be around them.

“ignorance is bliss” the classic saying.

not knowing meant you could be inside your own experience without your brain running commentary on the outside of it simultaneously.

and you can’t go back.

once you see it, it’s seen. once you understand the mechanism, you cannot ununderstand it.

people describe this kind of awareness as liberation. enlightenment. finally seeing clearly.

what they don’t describe is the isolation it creates.

because the more aware you become, the harder it gets to relate to people still fully inside the experience you’ve stepped outside of.

they’re watching the movie. you’re watching the movie and the projection equipment and the people watching the movie.

it’s like plato’s allegory of the cave.

they’re enjoying the party. you’re observing the party.

you’re not better or smarter by doing that either. you know that fact.

you’re just outside something they’re still inside.

and you can’t bridge that gap just by wanting to.

## THE ONLY WAY THROUGH

here’s the part nobody likes.

you can’t go backwards. but you can’t stop here either.

because partial awareness is the worst state to live in.

you see enough to ruin the experience but not enough to transcend what you’ve seen.

you see the masks. you haven’t accepted yet that everyone is masks. including you. and that masks aren an adaptation to the environment you live in.

a survival method.

you analyze the performances yet you haven’t accepted yet that life is performance. and that performance doesn’t make something less real.

you notice the wounds in everyone.

you haven’t arrived yet at the understanding that wounds are how humans grow, and that everyone in front of you is doing their best with hardware they didn’t choose and didn’t fully design.

real awareness, the kind that stops being a curse and starts being something useful, requires going all the way through.

not stopping at “i see the patterns.”

getting to “i see the patterns, i contain the same patterns, and we’re all doing this together, and that’s okay.”

it’s a longer road.

but partial awareness is where people get stuck and where i was stuck for longer than i like to admit.

aware enough to suffer from what they see. not yet aware enough to sit with it without being undone by it.

## THE BOTTOM LINE

awareness is a switch that only turns one direction.

the gap between stimulus and response that self-awareness creates changes everything about how experience lands.

movies become constructions. parties become performances. conversations become diagnostics. your own emotions become data points.

and you lose the ability to just exist in something without part of you hovering above it, taking notes.

maybe the other side of total awareness is acceptance. compassion. the ability to see all of it clearly and still choose to be there anyway.

to see someone’s wounds and connect with them because of that, not despite it.

to watch the performance and still find something real inside it.

to know you’re wearing a mask and still let someone close enough to see underneath it.

i don’t know. i’m not fully there yet.

still somewhere in the middle.

but i think the way out isn’t less awareness.

it’s awareness that’s learned to be gentle with what it sees.
