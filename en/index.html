Academic Profile & Research Vision

Metabolic Flexibility, Adipose Dysfunction, and Biostatistical Modeling in Cardiometabolic Disorders

I completed my Ph.D. in Nutrition and Dietetics in November 2024 at Ankara University Graduate School of Health Sciences with my doctoral dissertation titled "Evaluation of Diet-Related Risk Factors According to the Severity of Non-Alcoholic Fatty Liver Disease (NAFLD)".

My research focuses on lifespan cardiometabolic disorders, pediatric and adult obesity diagnostic metrics (Triponderal Mass Index, BMI z-scores), intermittent fasting (IF), time-restricted eating (TRE), ultra-processed foods (UPF), and systemic biomarkers (zonulin, total antioxidant capacity, oxidative stress). I employ R programming for multivariable biostatistical modeling, integrate Artificial Intelligence (AI) & LLM frameworks into biomedical data analytics, hold the Laboratory Animal Care & Use Accreditation for translational research, and actively contribute to COST Action CA23110 (INFLAMomx) focusing on multi-omics data integration in metabolic inflammation.

Pedagogy & Teaching

Undergraduate Courses

Courses taught at Akdeniz University Faculty of Health Sciences, Department of Nutrition and Dietetics:

BES 200 • Undergraduate Module (Fall / Spring)

Computer and AI Applications in Nutrition & Dietetics

The impact of artificial intelligence and digital technologies on nutritional science; practical tools for dietetic practice, nutrient analysis, client education, and hands-on case workshops.

🎯 Core Topics: Impact of AI on nutrition and dietetics, digital tools for dietary analysis and meal planning, using AI for client communication and education, efficient scientific literature search, ethics and limitations of AI in dietetics.

💡 Classroom Practice & AI: Exploring the impact of AI on nutrition science and emerging trends through extensive hands-on workshops, real-life dietetic case scenarios, and practical digital tool applications.

🚀 Outcomes: Consciously and ethically utilize AI and computer tools in dietetic practice, navigate digital food databases, and integrate technology into client management workflows.

BES 130 • Undergraduate Module (Spring)

Psychosocial Aspects of Nutrition

Evidence-based analysis of the biopsychosocial, cultural, and environmental dynamics shaping individual and societal eating behaviors.

🎯 Core Topics: Appetite mechanisms and food choice, disordered eating and eating disorders (anorexia, bulimia, BED), obesity and body image, psychosocial perspectives on GLP-1 therapies, nutritional fanaticism and orthorexia.

💡 Classroom Practice & AI: Psychosocial dimensions of GLP-1 popularity, dietary fanaticism, body image stigma, and psychometric screening tools analysis.

🚀 Outcomes: Analyze eating behaviors through a biopsychosocial framework, identify eating disorder risk factors, and develop client-centered mindful nutrition approaches.

BES 131 • Undergraduate Module (Fall)

Nutrition, Media & Science Communication

Critical evaluation of nutrition claims in media, identifying misinformation, and translating evidence-based scientific research into accessible public communication.

🎯 Core Topics: Media literacy, social media algorithms, influencer marketing, diet myths, food advertising tactics, fake news and misinformation analysis.

💡 Classroom Practice & AI: Evidence-based content creation on social media, scientific fact-checking workshops, and publication of 'Social Media Guidelines for Dietitians' brochures and presentations (Fall 2024-2025).

🚀 Outcomes: Critically evaluate nutrition news and viral diet trends, produce ethical and accessible science communication materials tailored for diverse target audiences.

BES 339 • Undergraduate Module (Fall)

Principles of Nutrition and Popular Diets

Physiological and metabolic foundations of diet planning combined with evidence-based critical analysis of current popular dietary trends.

🎯 Core Topics: Evolution of human nutrition, energy expenditure models, diet culture criticism, Ketogenic diet protocols, Intermittent Fasting (IF/TRE), longevity nutrition, and dietary myths.

💡 Classroom Practice & AI: AI-powered Google NotebookLM digital notebook; Critical brainstorming on 'Is obesity a disease?', 'Is calorie counting effective?', 'What is the ideal diet?' with student podcast submissions broadcast in class (Fall 2024-2025).

🎤 Student Seminars & Presentations: Circadian Rhythm, Sleep & Nutrition • Food Addiction & Cravings • Psychosocial Factors in Diet Adherence • GLP-1 Agonists & Nutrition • Personalized Nutrition • AI in Nutritional Science (Fall 2024-2025).

🚀 Outcomes: Distinguish evidence-based nutritional protocols from commercial fad diets, manage long-term metabolic and behavioral outcomes of popular diets.

AI Interactive Notebook (NotebookLM) →

BES 326 • Undergraduate Module (Spring)

Immune System and Immunonutrition

Immunological physiology, nutrient-immune interactions, inflammatory pathways, and targeted immunonutrition strategies in chronic diseases.

🎯 Core Topics: Innate and adaptive immunity, mucosal barrier integrity (GALT), key immunonutrients (Omega-3, glutamine, arginine, selenium, zinc, vitamin D), one-carbon metabolism, autoimmune disorders.

💡 Classroom Practice & AI: AI-powered Google NotebookLM digital notebook; Academic class debate on 'Should We Use Dietary Supplements?' (Spring 2024-2025); Department-wide Nutrition and Dietetics Quiz Bowl (Spring 2025-2026).

🎤 Student Seminars & Presentations: Food Additives & Immune System • Maternal Diet & Immune Epigenetics • Probiotics & Immunity • Ketogenic Diet & Cancer (Spring 2025-2026); Fasting Diets & Immunity • Food Additives • Ketogenic Diet (Spring 2024-2025).

🚀 Outcomes: Understand molecular mechanisms of immunonutrients and develop targeted nutritional protocols for acute and chronic inflammatory conditions.

AI Interactive Notebook (NotebookLM) →

BES 317 • Undergraduate Module (Fall)

Medical Nutrition Therapy Practice I

Evidence-based clinical diet therapy and case management in cardiometabolic and gastrointestinal diseases using the Nutrition Care Process (NCP).

🎯 Core Topics: Antropometric and clinical biochemistry interpretation, obesity management, glycemic control and carbohydrate counting in Type 2 Diabetes, CVD, hypertension, Mediterranean and DASH diets, GI disorders (celiac, IBD, GERD).

💡 Classroom Practice & AI: Comprehensive Mediterranean and DASH Diet Workshop: Cross-national portion dynamics, local biodiversity, and analytical exchange list modeling beyond simple caloric restriction (Fall 2025-2026).

🚀 Outcomes: Prescribe patient-tailored medical nutrition therapies in complex metabolic conditions and monitor clinical outcomes with precision.

BES 318 • Undergraduate Module (Spring)

Medical Nutrition Therapy Practice II

Advanced medical nutrition therapy planning and clinical decision-making in oncology, nephrology, hepatology, and critical surgical care.

🎯 Core Topics: Cancer cachexia and oncologic nutrition, acute/chronic renal disease and dialysis management, liver disorders (MASLD/MASH, cirrhosis), pancreatitis, metabolic stress management in burns and surgery.

💡 Classroom Practice & AI: Interactive AI-powered Case Simulations tailored for patients undergoing GLP-1 Receptor Agonist therapy (Spring 2025-2026).

🚀 Outcomes: Formulate integrated clinical nutrition care plans for severe multi-organ pathologies within interdisciplinary healthcare teams.

BES 420 • Undergraduate Module (Spring)

Enteral and Parenteral Nutrition in Adults

Clinical nutrition support, artificial feeding routes, formula selection, and ESPEN guideline-based case management in critically ill and compromised adult patients.

🎯 Core Topics: Nutritional risk screening (NRS-2002, MUST, GLIM), enteral access routes and formulations, parenteral nutrition (TPN) solutions, surgical ERAS protocols, complication prevention.

💡 Classroom Practice & AI: AI-powered complex case simulations for clinical decision-making; In-depth literature case report analysis, student solution defense, and ESPEN guideline comparison (Spring 2024-2025 & 2025-2026).

🚀 Outcomes: Select optimal artificial feeding routes and formulations tailored to clinical status, anticipate and prevent metabolic complications in simulated case models.
