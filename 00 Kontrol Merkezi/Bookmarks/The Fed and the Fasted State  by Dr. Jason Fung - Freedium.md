Body fat is simply a store of calories. When we eat, insulin goes up, which tells our body to store calories (as body fat). When we don't eat (fasting), insulin goes down, which tells our body to burn those stored calories (body fat) The human body exists in one of two states — the fed state (insulin high, storing calories) and the fasted state (insulin low, burning calories). The body is either storing food energy or burning stored food energy but not both.

Insulin inhibits lipolysis and glycogenolysis, which is a fancy way of saying that high insulin levels blocks the body from burning fat or sugar. High insulin also increases de novo lipogenesis (the creation of new fat stores) and glycogen synthesis (creation of new glycogen, or sugar stores). This makes sense. When we eat, insulin goes up to tell our body to store those calories as sugar or fat, and not to burn our stores of sugar and fat.## [Conditioned Hunger](https://drjasonfung.medium.com/conditioned-hunger-a0e263dff155)

What happens if insulin levels are too high? That's not hard to predict. Don't overthink things.

> · *Insulin tells our body to store sugar. If insulin is too high, we store too much sugar. This is the disease known as type 2 diabetes.*

> · *Insulin tells our body to store fat. If insulin is too high, we store too much fat. This is the disease known as obesity.*

Our ability to store food energy (calories) is the reason we don't die in our sleep every single night. When we're not eating, such as during sleep, our body still requires energy for basic metabolism (brain, heart, kidneys etc.), and that food energy (calories) must come from storage (sugar or fat). The trigger is not excess calories. The trigger is insulin. If you eat 6 times a day, you spike insulin 6 times a day, which tells your body "Please put calories into body fat" 6 times a day. That is a recipe for heartbreak and heartburn.

Fasting is simply the fastest and most efficient method of reducing insulin, which is very beneficial if insulin levels are high, as they are in people who are overweight or suffer from pre-diabetes or type 2 diabetes.

Calorie restriction does not necessarily lower insulin levels. This is why fasting succeeds where calorie restriction sometimes fails.

**Tip — Insulin blocks fat burning by switching between FASTED and FED state. Fasting is the most efficient method of lowering insulin.**

#### Can you skip a meal?

Why not? If you don't eat, your body will simply take the calories it needs from its stored calories — body fat.

In the 1970s, naughty boys were often sent to bed without dinner. I don't know personally because I was a very good boy. This regular 20 hour fast didn't cause any harm. Yes, they were hungry, and hopefully learned their lesson, but it wasn't unhealthy.

Today, if you skip a meal, some people react like you just spit on the Pope. Oh, My, God. You must always eat breakfast, even if it is a Krispy Kreme donut! Oh, My, God. I can't believe you have been awake for almost 2 minutes and haven't started cramming food into your mouth. Call 911! Oh, by the way — why can't you lose weight?

Don't over-complicate things. You can't burn body fat if you are eating. You can only burn body fat when you are NOT eating. There's no way around this bloody obvious fact. Therefore, spend more time NOT eating (fasting). It's not that difficult.

The advice to eat constantly to lose weight ***sounds*** really stupid, because it ***is*** really stupid. Eating more frequently generally means eating more overall as shown in [The Adventist Health Study 2,](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28701389/) involving over 50,000 people. This is intuitive and easy to understand. Eat constantly to lose weight? How does that work? Eating cannot cause weight loss. You can only lose weight when you don't eat.

However, skipping breakfast and pushing your meals later, as we noted earlier, has several disadvantages. So, to maximize fasting time, it would be ideal to skip dinner rather than breakfast. But that's not the end of the story. Skipping dinner is very hard, from a social standpoint because it does fit into most people's work/ life schedule.

I eat dinner with my family most nights, and we rarely eat breakfast together as a family, because we're often rushing to school and work. Skipping breakfast and eating dinner fits my social schedule. Eating breakfast and skipping dinner does not. It's a trade-off. Skipping dinner is better from a physiologic standpoint, but much worse from a social standpoint. If it gets too hard socially, then I'll eventually stop. Trying to shoehorn an eating schedule into an incompatible life schedule is weight loss suicide.

Skipping breakfast allows me to use a default eating schedule of two meals per day, instead of three. When I do eat breakfast, I feel really full, so I can't eat lunch. This is the power of defaulting to a lower number of meals per day. Eating twice a day is normal for me, and therefore does not require willpower or thought. Over time, I eat less, because of the default setting.

There's nothing wrong with eating breakfast. There's nothing wrong with not eating breakfast. Breakfast is technically the meal that breaks your fast, and you can break your fast anytime you like, not necessarily in the early morning. After all, does anybody yell at you when you eat brunch? But you still want to get a nice, long fasting period overnight to maximize the time you are allowing your body to use stored calories (body fat). Ideally, you'd eat a large breakfast and a small dinner, which is entirely in line with the advice to avoid eating late discussed earlier.

**Tip — Regular Fasting Establishes Expectations and sets up automatic weight loss.**
