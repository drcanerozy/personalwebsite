Methods: "Firstly, based on the connotation of AI nutritionists, 14 keywords (nutrition, diet, smart, dietary, the application, health, intake, monitoring, digital, mobile phone, web service, software, tool, and platform) were determined from the aspect of possible research objects, research contents, and presentation forms. Subsequently, AI nutritionist- related studies published over the past ten years (2012–2023) were retrieved from the PubMed database by combining different keywords according to experience. After the primary screening, a total of 17143 records were obtained, and 4481 records were then identified after removing records from informal publications. Subsequently, following a series of analyses on the title and abstract and literature extension mining, 177 target studies were finally obtained and analyzed. Detailed information on the 177 items can be found in the supporting information (Table S1). In terms of research contents, these AI nutritionist-related studies can be classified into three types: food recognition and dietary record, nutrient/recipe recommendation, and health monitoring. The information-bearing form of these research contents was subsequently analyzed. It was found that the AI nutritionists-related software had mainly five application forms: toolkit/package, webserver, APP, soft­ ware, and device. The proportion of three types of research content and five software forms was determined to further investigate the underlying rules (Fig. 1D and E)."
Findings: "The above statistical analysis results revealed some history and the development rules of current AI nutritionists. (1) Dietary record-related studies accounted for the largest portion (69.49 %) of research content (Fig. 1D), which was closely associated with the initial functionality (to facilitate the recording of dietary status) and development process of AI nutritionists. (2) The research related to AI nutritionists has shown obvious growth since 2012. However, there has been some fluctuation in the number of studies in adjacent years. There was a noticeable surge in 2014, but a temporary slowdown was observed between 2016 and 2018, followed by an upward trend. The statistical analysis of the data suggests that AI nutritionists have shown continuous growth both in terms of functionality and quantity. The short-term fluctuations may be attrib­ uted to the inconsistency between the implementation cycle and pub­ lication time of the projects (Fig. 1F). (3) Most of the articles are associated with keywords such as “Nutrition, Health, Diet, Intake, Di­ etary”. On one hand, this is because the majority of current research topics related to AI nutritionists are closely related to basic dietary be­ haviors. On the other hand, the vast majority of publications related to AI nutritionists focus on the content and functionality of the research subjects, with very few providing detailed descriptions of their software application forms. This also indicates the lack of research on the methods and software frameworks of AI nutritionists. (4) Most AI nu­ tritionists did not use the AI algorithm in the strictest sense; Among the examples of using AI algorithms, artificial neural networks (ANN) and its variants are the most common (Figs. 1A and 3A). However, the application of ANN in the food science field is still in its early stage compared to other fields [20]. In some studies, ANN can be used to predict the relationship between dietary patterns and chronic diseases [21] and to effectively improve the nutritional status of different pop­ ulations [22]. In addition, we listed representative events in Fig. 1B and C. It can be seen that the development of AI nutritionists in terms of algorithms is progressive, evolving from simple data recording to the formation of a certain scale and specific functionality of databases, from simple equations and calculations to machine learning, and ultimately applying the latest deep learning (DL) algorithms. In terms of applica­ tion form, it starts with simple computational tools, then develops into network-based databases and web-based systems; comprehensive soft­ ware combining multiple sensing devices has recently emerged.In the above-summarized aspects, most of the researchers conduct nutritional projects based on existing AI nutritionist software and plat­ forms. In addition to focusing on these evaluation results, the imple­ mentation strategy of AI nutritionists corresponding to physical entities is the foundation and core of future development. However, most nutrition-related software fail to leverage AI technology in its true sense; instead, they only perform data analysis and comparison to provide relevant feedback [24,68,69]. Moreover, even for those equipped with AI technology, the implementation process was usually not described thoroughly, but only used AI technology to conduct some case studies.  Therefore, it is necessary to analyze and explore the implementation process of these AI nutritionists, which could lay the foundation for developing more high-quality AI nutritionists. The implementation of AI nutritionists is primarily achieved through three forms: data acquisition, algorithm selection, and forms and architecture. Data are the prereq­ uisite for AI nutritionist establishment, algorithm selection and appli­ cation are the core, while forms and architecture determine the final application scenario and intended audience. The subsequent analyses were conducted from these three aspects.Currently, most AI nutritionists mainly obtain data through retro­ spective data collection, user input, and active monitoring. Retrospec­ tive data collection primarily relies on existing public databases, publicly published literature, and consensus data from various in­ stitutions and organizations.In AI nutritionists, there are two situations where algorithms are needed: (1) use the existing AI algorithms to establish models for pre­ diction and recommendations, and (2) input sample data to estimate the results and provide recommendations based on existing rules and con­ ventional calculation formulas"
Conclusions: "Several points merit further discussion in light of the preceding discussion. (1) The majority of AI nutritionists are merely tools with limited recording capabilities, and their intelligence level is low.  Although such programs can spare humans from some labor-intensive jobs, there is room for improvement in determining methods to augment their intelligence. Surprisingly, some applications developed in the computing field, for example, Food2k [30], have shown substantial improvement on this point. However, how to endow these applications with the meaning of food nutrition is another important issue. (2) Multicenter and individualized precision nutrition is the future trend.  Dietary intake among different groups and regions is remarkably different, manifested in staple food intake and trace element ingestion.  On this issue, the individualization and precision of nutrition issues are more obvious than the individualization in the medical field. Therefore, multi-center and multi-level model analysis is particularly necessary. (3) Personalized nutrition needs to incorporate more artificial intelligence models to evaluate molecular-level behaviors4) AI nutritionists may experience non-technical barriers while solving specific real-world problems, such as how to combine precise nutritional assessment and personalized di­ etary recommendations, and how to integrate expert experience to trigger practical value, avoiding a disconnect between theoretical guidance and practical implementation. Furthermore, it is important to explore how to develop an evaluation system to measure the contribu­ tions of AI nutritionists and increase their reputation among professional nutritionists and users. (5) Concerning technology, IoT combined with AI is an obvious trend in future development. Sensor technology has developed rapidly. Integrating diverse wearable sensors and environ­ mental sensors into IoT systems enables more accurate and objective measurement of food intake and behavior, hence providing a database for continuous big data collection and time-series-based AI prediction [16]. (6) Regarding implementation forms, modular and platform-crossed frameworks are the future trends. The optimal frame­ work for AI dietitians captures data through the APP or sensor end, stores them in the cloud, analyzes and visualizes them via webserver on the desktop end, and concludes with real-time receipt and feedback messages on the APP. (7) In the development of AI nutritionists, the handling of personal privacy is insufficiently considered, especially concerning Nutrient/recipe recommendations and health monitoring.  Despite being a societal issue under the wave of AI and big data, personal privacy is not limited to the field of nutrition. However, it is undeniable that inappropriate handling of personal privacy in AI nutritionists can affect both the accessibility of the tool and the accuracy of their methods. In future research, techniques such as data anonymization and blockchain technology, combined with robust privacy protemechanisms formed through social power institutions may help AI nu­ tritionists mitigate this situationThis article first introduces the definition and connotation of AI nutritionists in a broad sense. Following a series of retrospective eval­ uations of previously published articles, the current stage of AI nutri­ tionists’ development was given. Next, the implementation status and characteristics of AI nutritionists are introduced in three main aspects (food recognition and dietary record, nutrient/recipe recommendations, and health monitoring). Finally, the implementation strategies of AI nutritionists in terms of algorithms and applications are summarized and analyzed. In addition, this essay predicts the future development pattern of AI nutritionists, which is believed to provide thorough guidelines for performing AI-related research on the subject of food nutrition and help to bridge the gap between food nutrition research findings and the application of new AI products"
Limitations: "Firstly, a significant number of AI nutritionist projects do not offer specific project and server addresses, which was a problem for users and a loss for researchers. Therefore, it is essential to improve the management and planning of AI nutritionist projects, particularly with respect to standardizing domain names and determining service modSecondly, certain projects have limited availability, resulting in times out of service in which they are inaccessible or cease to function.  Moreover, a significant proportion of projects are not updated in a timely manner, resulting in the waste of resources during the develop­ ment phase without the delivery of continued value. Therefore, to completely fulfill their practical value, AI nutritionist initiatives on technological support and upgrades must be increased"

The AI nutritionist we speak of here is not a nutrition robot or a simple artificial intelligence algorithm in the traditional sense, but a more comprehen­ sive and extensive application of intelligent software that transformed from theoretical methods in the field of nutrition. They help us acquire a comprehensive understanding of the complex and multi-level relation­ ship among food, dietary behavior, metabolic regulation, and energy balance.
