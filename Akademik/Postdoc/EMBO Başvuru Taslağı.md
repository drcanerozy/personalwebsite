## Araştırma Sorusu

### Temel Soru
Kalori kısıtlaması (CR) sırasında serum T3'teki düşüş ve kahverengi yağ dokusu (BAT) termogenik kapasitesindeki değişim, bireysel adaptif termogenez büyüklüğünü ve 12 aylık kilo geri kazanımını açıklıyor mu?

### İkincil Sorular
1. Adaptif termogenez büyük olan kişilerde (thrifty fenotip) adiposit boyutu CR sonrasında daha mı büyük kalıyor? (adipoz yeniden şekillenme yetersizliği / "catch-up fat" zemini)
2. BAT kapasitesini koruyanlar (∆UCP-1 ↑ veya sabit) daha az adaptif termogenez mi yaşıyor? — BAT bir "spendthrift" koruyucu faktörü mü?
3. ∆T3 + ∆BAT + adaptif termogenez indeksi birlikte 12. ay kilosunu grup ortalamasından bağımsız öngörüyor mu?

### Neden Özgün
BATON, bu soruyu yanıtlamak için gereken nadir ölçüm kombinasyonuna sahip: REE (indirekt kalorimetri), serum T3/T4/TSH, BAT kapasitesi (UCP-1 proteini + PET-CT uptake + BAT hacmi), adiposit boyutu (histoloji) ve 12 aylık kilo takibi — hepsi aynı kohort, aynı zaman noktaları. Bu değişkenleri birlikte kullanan başka bir insan çalışması yok.

BATON'un primer sorusu BAT adaptasyonunu tanımlamak; "kimin daha fazla uyum gösterdiği ve neden" sorusu sorulmamış. Bu, analizin BATON ekibine katkısı.

### Teorik Arka Plan (vault notlarından)
- **Catch-up Fat mekanizması:** CR sonrası yeniden beslenme döneminde iskelet kası D3 deiyodinazı (DIO3) upregüle ediyor → lokal fonksiyonel hipotiroidi → RMR baskılanması → yağ tercihli dolum. Kanda TSH ve T4 normal görünse de T3 düşük kalabiliyor.
- **Açlıkta T3 baskılanması:** Cagigas et al. 2025 — 9.8 günlük su orucunda serbest T3 3.29 → 2.05 pg/mL (p<0.0001); BHB artışı ve BCAA salınımıyla senkronize. CR adaptasyonunda benzer eksen aktif.
- **Bireysel farklılık katmanı:** DIO2 gen polimorfizmi (Thr92Ala) T3 duyarlılığını belirliyor — aynı T3 düşüşüne farklı metabolik yanıt. BATON'da genotipleme yok ama serum T3 değişkenliği dolaylı proxy olarak kullanılabilir.

### Bölüm 2 — Summary and Biological Significance (~200 kelime)

Weight regain following successful caloric restriction (CR) remains the central unresolved challenge in obesity management. A key determinant of regain risk is the magnitude of adaptive thermogenesis (AT) — the suppression of resting energy expenditure beyond what lean mass loss alone predicts. Individuals vary substantially in AT magnitude, but the mechanistic drivers of this inter-individual variation are poorly understood.

Two thermogenic systems emerge as primary candidates: the hypothalamic-pituitary-thyroid axis, whose suppression during CR reduces peripheral T3 availability and skeletal muscle metabolic rate; and brown adipose tissue (BAT), whose thermogenic capacity after CR may buffer or amplify the overall energy expenditure deficit. Adipocyte remodelling may additionally encode a "tissue memory" of prior adiposity, independently sustaining conditions for fat re-accumulation.

The BATON trial (NCT06878989, n=30) uniquely combines, in the same cohort at matched time points, measures of REE, serum thyroid hormones (T3, T4, TSH), BAT thermogenic capacity (PET-CT uptake, UCP-1 protein content), adipocyte size, and 12-month weight follow-up. No published study has integrated these measures to phenotype thrifty versus spendthrift metabolic responses after CR. This secondary analysis will generate the first human evidence linking thyroid axis and BAT changes to AT heterogeneity and weight regain, providing effect size estimates for a future large-scale trial. *(~190 kelime)*

### Bölüm 3 — Background and Reason (~500 kelime)

The failure to maintain weight loss is a near-universal outcome in obesity treatment. Across dietary interventions, 80–95% of participants regain a substantial proportion of lost weight within two years. Adaptive thermogenesis — the metabolic compensation that reduces total energy expenditure beyond what changes in body composition predict — is mechanistically central to this regain. AT of 100–200 kcal/day has been documented after 10% weight loss, persisting for years, and its magnitude correlates with the rate of subsequent weight regain.

What determines how much AT an individual undergoes remains an open question. Two biological systems are particularly implicated but have not been examined simultaneously in the same human cohort.

**Thyroid axis:** Circulating triiodothyronine (T3) declines during CR, reducing resting metabolic rate. Recent evidence extends this beyond the systemic level: skeletal muscle upregulates type 3 deiodinase (DIO3) during energy deficit, locally inactivating T3 to reverse-T3 and creating a state of intramuscular hypothyroidism even when systemic TSH remains normal. This local mechanism further suppresses muscle contractile kinetics, promotes type II to type I fibre conversion, and reduces protein turnover — collectively lowering energy expenditure. A polymorphism in the type 2 deiodinase gene (DIO2 Thr92Ala) further modulates individual sensitivity to circulating T3, providing a genetic basis for the observed inter-individual variation in AT. Serum T3 change during CR has been proposed as a clinical proxy for this local thermogenic suppression, but its relationship to simultaneously measured AT magnitude has not been formally tested.

**Brown adipose tissue:** BAT thermogenesis contributes meaningfully to total energy expenditure in humans under cold exposure and possibly under dietary stimulation. Weight loss can alter BAT volume, glucose uptake capacity, and UCP-1 content in opposing directions depending on the model studied. Whether individuals who preserve BAT thermogenic capacity after CR demonstrate attenuated AT — that is, whether BAT acts as a "spendthrift" buffer — has not been examined in a cohort where BAT is directly measured alongside whole-body energy expenditure.

**Adipocyte remodelling** adds a third layer: adipocytes that fail to reduce in size after CR (or rapidly refill during refeeding) may reflect an ECM-driven mechanical memory that independently maintains conditions for lipid re-accumulation, irrespective of hormonal signals.

The BATON trial at the University of Granada offers an exceptional opportunity to address these gaps. It is, to our knowledge, the only completed human CR trial that simultaneously measures serum thyroid hormones, BAT thermogenic parameters by PET-CT and tissue analysis, direct adipocyte size by histology, indirect calorimetry-derived REE, and 12-month body weight follow-up, in the same participants. This secondary analysis will not be designed to provide definitive causal inference — sample size precludes this — but will deliver the first integrated human evidence on these relationships and robust effect size estimates for a future adequately powered investigation. *(~460 kelime)*

### Bölüm 4 — Key Aim and Objectives (~300 kelime)

**Overall aim:** To characterise the thrifty metabolic phenotype after caloric restriction in the BATON cohort by quantifying inter-individual variation in adaptive thermogenesis and testing its association with concurrent changes in thyroid axis activity, BAT thermogenic capacity, and adipocyte remodelling.

**Objective 1:** Calculate an adaptive thermogenesis index for each BATON participant by subtracting the expected REE change (derived from fat-free mass change using established prediction equations) from the measured REE change. Describe the distribution of AT across participants and identify thrifty versus spendthrift subgroups.

**Objective 2:** Test whether the magnitude of serum T3 decline during CR (∆T3 = CR-end minus baseline) predicts AT index, and whether this association holds after accounting for fat-free mass change.

**Objective 3:** Examine whether changes in BAT thermogenic capacity (∆BAT UCP-1 content, ∆BAT 18F-FDG uptake, ∆BAT volume) are inversely associated with AT index — testing the hypothesis that BAT preservation buffers whole-body thermogenic suppression.

**Objective 4:** Assess whether adipocyte size after CR correlates with AT index and T3 change, and whether AT index — with or without T3 and BAT parameters — predicts 12-month body weight above and beyond weight lost during CR.

**Secondary objective:** Quantify effect sizes for all associations to inform the design and sample size calculation of a future adequately powered prospective study, and to define the analytical framework for a work package in a subsequent MSCA Postdoctoral Fellowship proposal targeting the 2027 call. *(~230 kelime)*

### Bölüm 5 — Research Plan with Timeline (~1000 kelime)

**Phase 1 — Data Familiarisation and Methodological Consensus (Days 1–3)**

*Day 1:* Laboratory orientation and introduction to BATON team members. Review of the study database structure, variable codebook, and data collection procedures. Identification of the specific variables available for secondary analysis and any data access or anonymisation requirements to be resolved before analysis begins. Clarification of the current status of 12-month weight follow-up data.

*Day 2:* Discussion with PI regarding the methodology for AT index calculation. Key decision: which prediction equation to use for expected REE change given observed fat-free mass change (Müller et al. 2013 or Ravussin-group approach). Agreement on this approach is foundational, as the AT index definition drives all subsequent analyses. Setup of the R analytical environment with the agreed dataset. Initial data import, variable formatting, and missing data audit.

*Day 3:* Data cleaning, outlier detection, and descriptive statistics for all analysis variables. Calculation of the AT index for each CR-arm participant. Visualisation of the AT distribution. Assessment of whether a bimodal thrifty/spendthrift separation is observable or whether a continuous model is more appropriate. Review of outputs with PI.

**Phase 2 — Primary Analyses (Days 4–9)**

*Day 4:* Correlation analysis — ∆T3 (serum) versus AT index. Pearson and Spearman coefficients, scatter plot with 95% confidence band, effect size estimation. Simple linear regression with AT index as outcome. Preliminary interpretation with PI.

*Day 5:* Correlation analyses — ∆BAT UCP-1 content, ∆BAT 18F-FDG uptake, and ∆BAT volume versus AT index. Assessment of whether different BAT metrics provide convergent or divergent signals. Exploratory comparison of AT magnitude between participants showing BAT preservation versus BAT decline.

*Day 6:* Adipocyte size analysis — correlation between post-CR adipocyte size (and ∆adipocyte size) and AT index; correlation between adipocyte size and ∆T3. Visual exploration of whether participants with larger post-CR adipocytes cluster in the thrifty subgroup.

*Day 7:* Fatmax change (∆maximal fat oxidation rate) versus AT index. Additionally, bivariate analysis of ∆T3 and ∆BAT-UCP1 to assess whether thyroid axis and BAT changes are co-regulated or independent during CR.

*Day 8:* Weight regain analysis. Primary model: AT index as predictor of 12-month body weight (adjusted for weight at CR-end). If sample size permits, a second model adding ∆T3 as a second predictor. Given n constraints, models will be kept to a maximum of two predictors and results explicitly framed as preliminary effect size estimates.

*Day 9:* Sensitivity analyses. AT index will be recalculated using an alternative prediction equation to assess robustness. Missing data handled by complete-case analysis with a sensitivity check using last-observation-carried-forward. All analytical code documented in RMarkdown for reproducibility and shared with the host group.

**Phase 3 — Synthesis and Future Planning (Days 10–12)**

*Day 10:* Figure preparation — forest of individual AT values, scatter plots for primary associations, summary figure integrating the thyroid-BAT-AT-regain model. Draft methods section for potential manuscript. Final code review and documentation.

*Day 11:* Informal results presentation to EFFECTS group. Discussion of findings in the context of REGANE (NCT07315659) — identifying how a future adequately powered work package could test the same relationships with n>200 and 36-month follow-up. REGANE protocol review to inform MSCA-PF framing.

*Day 12:* Structured meeting with PI — co-authorship agreement for outputs arising from the secondary analysis, timeline for a joint manuscript, roadmap for the MSCA-PF 2027 proposal integrating BATON findings as preliminary data. Finalisation of written visit report.

**Expected outputs:** (a) Preliminary dataset with AT index and association statistics, ready for manuscript preparation; (b) Reproducible R code shared with host group; (c) Defined analytical framework and effect size estimates for MSCA-PF work package; (d) First draft of BATON secondary analysis methods section. *(~750 kelime — form sınırı içinde)*

### Bölüm 9 — Justification of the Choice of This Laboratory (~200 kelime)

The EFFECTS-262 Research Group at the University of Granada is, to our knowledge, the only European group that has simultaneously collected, in a controlled CR trial, direct BAT measurements (PET-CT glucose uptake, UCP-1 protein content), adipocyte histology, whole-body indirect calorimetry, thyroid hormones, and longitudinal weight follow-up in the same participants. The secondary analysis I propose requires precisely this combination; no alternative dataset exists.

Professor Sánchez-Delgado's group possesses whole-room calorimetry infrastructure not available at Akdeniz University, and their established protocols for metabolic phenotyping during dietary interventions represent a methodological standard directly relevant to my own ongoing clinical research. His Ramón y Cajal position and FWCI of 1.5 reflect international standing well above field average.

This visit initiates a genuinely new collaboration: no prior joint publications or grant activities exist between the EFFECTS group and Akdeniz University. The timing — with BATON data in analysis and REGANE (NCT07315659, n=212) in early recruitment — is optimal: the proposed secondary analysis generates preliminary evidence immediately, while laying the groundwork for integration into REGANE's longitudinal dataset through a future MSCA Postdoctoral Fellowship. *(~190 kelime)*

### Bölüm 13 — Current Work at Home Lab (~500 kelime)

I am a research assistant (Arş. Gör. Dr.) in the Department of Nutrition and Dietetics at Akdeniz University, Antalya, Turkey, with concurrent clinical involvement at the university's endocrinology clinic. My research programme centres on two intersecting areas: the metabolic consequences of dietary pattern modification — particularly intermittent fasting, time-restricted eating (TRE), and caloric restriction protocols — and the role of adipose tissue biology in obesity pathophysiology.

My primary active clinical study is a registered three-arm randomised controlled trial comparing six meals per day, three meals per day, and TRE (eight-hour eating window) in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus. Primary outcomes include glycaemic control (HbA1c, fasting glucose, postprandial glucose excursions) and secondary metabolic parameters (insulin resistance indices, lipid profiles). This trial is currently in the data analysis and manuscript preparation phase. Methodologically, this study has revealed the importance of metabolic phenotyping for understanding individual responses to eating pattern interventions — a limitation I am specifically seeking to address through the proposed EMBO exchange, by acquiring the indirect calorimetry-based Fatmax and AT index methodologies used by the EFFECTS group.

In parallel, I am developing a theoretical and narrative framework on personalised intermittent fasting and adipose tissue remodelling, with specific attention to the refeeding phase — a neglected transition between caloric restriction and weight maintenance that may represent a mechanistic window for intervention. This work draws on the emerging literature on catch-up fat, ECM remodelling, and tissue metabolic memory, and is directly related to the biological questions I propose to investigate in the BATON dataset.

I maintain active membership in three COST Actions: CA24166 (INFLAMomx, WG1/WG3/WG4), CA23110 (INFOGUT, WG3/WG4/WG5), and CA25129 — networks that position my work within European metabolic disease and nutrition research communities.

### Bölüm 14 — Benefit for Home Lab (~200 kelime)

The primary methodological benefit to my home institution is acquisition of the EFFECTS group's protocols for indirect calorimetry-based metabolic phenotyping: specifically, the measurement and calculation of maximal fat oxidation rate (Fatmax) and adaptive thermogenesis index. These measurements are not currently performed at Akdeniz University, and integrating them into my active T2DM meal frequency RCT would substantially strengthen its secondary outcomes — enabling characterisation of metabolic flexibility responses to TRE and meal frequency manipulation, beyond the glycaemic control measures currently collected.

The secondary benefit is methodological: the analytical approach to AT index calculation and the mixed-effects modelling pipeline developed during the BATON secondary analysis will be directly transferable to my own dataset, allowing me to identify thrifty versus spendthrift responders within my T2DM cohort for the first time.

A longer-term benefit to the home institution is the establishment of an active bilateral research relationship with the EFFECTS group, facilitating future joint grant applications, student mobility, and collaborative access to PET-CT metabolic imaging infrastructure not available in the Antalya region. *(~185 kelime)*
