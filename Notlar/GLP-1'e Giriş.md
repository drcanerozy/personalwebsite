> > GLP-1 receptor agonists, including semaglutide and tirzepatide, are injectable medications that mimic GLP-1, a naturally occurring hormone in our bodies, which increases insulin production in response to elevated blood glucose levels. This medication was approved for the management of type 2 diabetes mellitus under the names Ozempic in 2017 and Mounjaro in 2022. 1 These medications also slow gastric emptying and decrease appetite by prolonging fullness and inhibiting areas of the brain involved in hunger and craving. 1 For this reason, in 2021, semaglutide was first approved by the FDA for chronic weight management for adults under the name Wegovy, 1 and tirzepatide was approved for weight loss in late 2023 under the name Zepbound. 2 These medications are approved for patients who have a BMI of 30 or higher, considered obese, or patients with a BMI of 27 or higher, considered overweight, with weight-related comorbidities such as dyslipidemia, type 2 diabetes mellitus, hypertension, or obstructive sleep apnea.  Weight loss with these medications is associated with a lower cardiovascular risk for myocardial infarctions and strokes; however, common side effects include GI upset, and in some cases, increased risk of pancreatitis, kidney failure, and thyroid cancer. 1 These potential side effects are an important consideration when deciding whether to prescribe these medications. Historically, Metformin was the sole first-line pharmacotherapy for type 2 diabetes. Other agents were used as adjuncts. However, ADA guidelines changed in 2022 to broaden first-line options to include SGLT-2 inhibitors and GLP-1 agonists, with or without Metformin. 3,4 This has led to a disparity in prescriptions. For example, two 50-year-old patients with new-onset diabetes and comorbid obesity and hyperlipidemia may receive different treatments. Patient A, with full coverage insurance, may be prescribed a GLP-1 agonist. Patient B, underinsured, may only qualify for Metformin due to coverage limits.
